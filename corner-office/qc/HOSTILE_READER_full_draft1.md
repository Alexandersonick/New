# HOSTILE READER: Full Draft 1

**Book:** *My CEO Husband Gave Her My Corner Office*. About 61,300 words, 30 chapters, dual first-person present tense (Pip 21 chapters, Adam 9).
**Pipeline:** v5.0.2. This pass follows page-craft-standards §7 (Hostile Reader Pass, including the repeat-reader briefing) and §5 (Ending on the Page). Breach rows and review-cause ranks come from reader-contract-gates §1 and §4.
**How I read it:** as an independent reader, cover to cover, using only `17_MANUSCRIPT.md`, the packaging copy in the brief, and `qc/repeat_reader_fingerprints.json`. I saw no outline, bible, architecture or chapter cards. Line numbers below are from `17_MANUSCRIPT.md`.

## Bottom line

| Layer | Result |
|---|---|
| First-time hostile reader | **FAIL.** One complaint maps to rank 11 (within-book repetition) at **medium** severity. The main cause is the fifteen-mornings backstory: it is told in full three times, with sentences repeated word for word. Phrase tics add to it. Rank 6 has two **high** complaints (billionaire signal, love triangle), but rank 6 is not a fail rank. Rank 10 (the climax clock and its money logic) is medium. Every other fail-rank complaint (1, 2, 3, 9, 13) is low. |
| Breach Gate | **No breach.** There is no cheating on the page, consent is clear, and repair is shown on the page as changed behaviour that costs the hero something. Two items are close to the line without crossing it: the grovel is diluted because the heroine is shown to be equally at fault, and the two-year estrangement is rooted in a mutual misunderstanding. |
| Ending §5 | Mostly passes. The climax is a scene, the denouement is present and the HEA is on the page. **Item 3 (register drop) fails:** the final paragraph is one 24-word sentence against a Ch30 mean of about 9.7 words. Kurt's defeat happens by email, off the page (low severity). |
| Repeat reader | **`could_write_the_same_book_review: true`, which is a FAIL. File it as `CATALOG_SAMENESS`.** The premise and set pieces are new: the crane heist, the viral demand, the company sale, and the heroine causing the darkest moment. The engine underneath is the house engine: hero's error, workshop retreat, tradesman accountability crew, "tell the bad part first" self-reporting, the heroine learning to ask, a work-floor climax with no clapping, a months-later shared bench, and the same voice tags. |
