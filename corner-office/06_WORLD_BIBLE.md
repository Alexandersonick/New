## Locked facts (continuity)

### Twodesk, Inc.
- Founded **2014** in Stan Sobczak's garage on the West Side of Grand Rapids. The two founders
  had two desks in that garage. Pip built the desks; Stan built neither (he did the welding).
- **Product:** factory-built backyard rooms, delivered on a lowboy trailer and set by crane in
  about forty minutes.
  - **The Corner:** 10 × 12 ft, the flagship, about $38,000 installed. Two glass walls meet with
    **no corner post**. A welded steel moment frame hidden in the walls (the crew calls it "the
    knee") carries the roof. Four lift points.
  - Also sold: the Eight (8 × 10) and the Long (10 × 16).
  - **Corner Lite:** Simone's cheaper redesign with a corner post, about $29,000. Pip hates it.
- **Unit 001:** the first Corner ever built, finished in the garage in **spring 2014**. Pip and Adam
  were married inside it in **June 2015** (a ten-minute ceremony: a judge, Stan, and Halina). It
  was installed in the northwest corner of the 4th floor at HQ in **2018**, craned in through a
  window bay that Pip designed to unbolt ("We put a door in the sky").
  - Pip owns it **personally**. It was never contributed to the company. The company leases it
    from her for $1 a year.
  - **The door rules.** On the inside of the door, in carpenter's pencil on birch ply, in both
    hands (2014):
    1. Nobody decides alone.
    2. Never spend a customer's money before you've cut their steel.
    3. Coffee at seven.
- **Coffee at seven:** for years the founders met in Unit 001 at 7 a.m. every working day, just
  the two of them. **Two years before page one**, Adam announced the Nashville plant at an
  all-hands meeting without deciding it with her. The next morning Pip stopped coming to coffee
  at seven, in silent protest. Adam came alone every morning for **three weeks**, then stopped.
  Each believes the other stopped first; Pip learns the truth late in Act 2.
- **HQ:** 4th floor of the old Lindqvist Furniture building on Monroe Avenue NW, by the Grand River.
- **Plant:** Clyde Park Avenue SW, Wyoming, MI. About 140 crew across two shifts. Lolo runs second
  shift. The yard is fenced and has a gravel set-down pad.
- **Nashville plant** (La Vergne, TN): opened fourteen months before page one. About 60 workers.
  Built for volume, it **cannot weld the knee**, so it can only build Corner Lite. It loses money
  every week. It is Adam's.
- **Cap table:** Adam 34%, Pip 33%, investors 33% (lead: Lakeshore Ventures, Ruth Baskin).
  - A sale needs **75%** of shares.
  - A **lock-up** bars the founders from transferring shares without board consent for 24 months
    after the last round (closed 20 months ago).
  - The press calls them "the billion-dollar couple": the last round valued the company at about
    $1.3B.
- **Acquirer:** Halvard Group of Minneapolis (CEO Kurt Lindahl). It wants the Corner brand and the
  order book. Its integration plan moves production to its own plants within eighteen months.
- **Cash:** about nine weeks at the midpoint. **Customer deposits (10%) sit in escrow** until steel is
  cut for that customer's unit (door rule 2, Pip's from 2014), so the order spike adds demand without
  adding spendable cash.
- **Adam's pledge:** last October he borrowed personally from Northgate Capital and put the money into
  payroll, pledging his shares. A covenant test on the company's cash is set for mid-March; after the
  Ch 20 interview the lender pulls it forward to **ten days**.

### Places

- **The Sobczaks:** Stan (74, retired tool-and-die maker, **alive**) and Halina (71), West Side,

## Procedural rules (Risk Tier: B, plausible-not-technical)
- **Crane removal:** a 100-ton mobile crane on Monroe Avenue NW needs a city street-closure permit.
  Pip's rigger, Kasprzak Rigging, holds a standing permit for the building, because it serviced
  the 2018 install. The window bay unbolts from inside. The lift happens at night, in under-25-mph
  wind. *Mild licence:* the permit stretch is acknowledged on the page in Ch 4–5 and costs Pip a
  city fine.
- **75% sale threshold and lock-up:** both are plausible terms of a private shareholders'
  agreement.
- **The crew vote** binds nobody legally. It is Pip's choice to ask, under door rule 1. A no would
  make her plan unworkable, because it depends on a twelve-week, six-day schedule.
