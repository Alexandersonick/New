# Blind batch 2: three candidate concepts (surface-stripped)

**Declared constants (the owner's packaging; fixed for every candidate).** The husband (CEO)
publicly gives the wife's founding office to a newly hired senior executive. He leans on her
emotionally; there is no physical affair. Asked why, he says the wife barely uses it. The wife
co-founded the company and owns a third of it. The book must deliver three things: the
boardroom learns whose idea everything was, he begs forgiveness, and they reconcile. These
are scored and reported, but under the owner's declaration they are not counted against the
candidate under H7. Every other axis is open.

Shared fact: the company makes rooms that are built in a factory and lifted into place by
crane. The office is the first such room ever built; she built it before the company existed.

---

## Candidate Z1 (revision of rejected X1)

- **POV / time:** two first-person narrators in present tense, mostly hers. **Opens in medias res:**
  at night, during her retaliation, a crane is lifting the office out of headquarters. Chapter 2
  goes back to the morning of the handover.
- **Bind (legal, not sentimental):** a pending sale to a large acquirer needs 75% of shareholders,
  so her third is a veto. A two-year lock-up bars her from selling her shares without board
  consent. She cannot sell out or walk out, and the sale cannot happen without her. The cost of
  staying is daily proximity to both of them.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 1–3):** overnight she has the office unbolted and lifted by
  crane into the factory yard. The price: an intern's video goes viral, the board moves to censure
  her, the acquirer flags "founder risk", and the husband is humiliated in front of the acquirer.
- **Midpoint (about 50%, in the boardroom); the product is causal here:** the viral video, a whole
  room lifted out of a building and driven away undamaged in one night, has produced thousands of
  deposit orders for her original design. The censure meeting turns into a meeting about that
  demand. The acquirer's diligence question about who invented the frame gets a one-line answer
  from the husband: hers. Then the finance chief lays out the trap. Deposits are a tenth of the
  price, building the orders needs working capital the company does not have, and there are nine
  weeks of cash. Only the acquirer will fund the build, and it would close the plant afterwards.
  **Her veto now starves the demand her own stunt created.** The flip comes from the product's
  defining property, not from anyone testifying.
- **Ally turn:** she and the new executive build an independent plan to fund and build the orders.
- **Lowest point. The heroine's own bad choice, a mirror of his fault:** under deadline she
  decides alone. In a live interview she announces that the company will not sell and will build
  the orders itself. She has told no one: not her husband, not the executive, not the board. The
  acquirer walks. The lender, spooked by "company in crisis" coverage, pulls the covenant test
  forward from nine weeks to ten days. The executive, blindsided exactly as the wife once was,
  resigns. Husband and wife have the worst fight of the book, and both are guilty. She asks him to
  go. *(He does not relapse. She does what he did.)*
- **Repair, staged without witnesses (dominant shape: accepting a consequence he could avoid):** the
  rhythm is announced in advance, done alone and unwatched, and taken on trust. He tells her what
  he is going to do and does it where nobody sees, and she chooses not to check. He ends the
  late-night confidences with the executive himself. He goes to the lender alone and tells them the
  cash crisis was his doing, which makes his own pledged shares the first thing they take if the
  plan fails. He apologizes, and he begs, once, in the removed office, with no audience. No family
  meal, no crowd, no testimony.
- **Climax (heroine-caused; he takes an independent, costly position that disagrees with her):** at
  shift change on the factory floor she puts her plan to a workforce vote: a pay deferral in exchange
  for equity from her own third. He stands up and **opposes part of it**: don't defer their pay; close
  the second plant instead. That plant is his own project and his pride, and she had protected it to
  spare him. They argue it out as equals in front of the room, and she has to decide on the spot
  whether to take his amendment. She takes it and calls the vote. Hands are counted, and it is not
  unanimous. Staging: workplace floor / the workforce / no evidence / a vote / a quiet tally.
- **Resolution:** reconciled, no epilogue, final scene within weeks. The office stays in the factory
  yard and is not moved home. **Ending image (object at rest):** two desks pushed together inside it.
- **Shape triple:** commission · accepting a consequence he could avoid · object at rest.
- **Emotional key:** the marriage itself. There is no dead parent and no legacy object.

## Candidate Z2

- **POV / time:** single first-person, past tense, **dual timeline**. Present-day chapters alternate
  with founding-years chapters set eight to twelve years earlier. **Opens before the crisis:** the
  night before the handover, at home.
- **Bind:** the founders' agreement has a deadlock clause. If the two founders disagree on a sale, a
  binding arbitration runs for 45 days, and neither may resign or sell during it.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 3):** instead of using her veto, she triggers deadlock
  arbitration. If she loses, the sale is forced and her shares are dragged along.
- **Midpoint:** the past-timeline chapters reveal that, years ago, she alone turned down on both
  their behalves an offer that would have saved his mother's house. She broke their rule first. The
  problem is reframed as mutual.
- **Lowest point:** the arbitrator's interim finding goes against her, and the husband's truthful
  testimony about the old refusal is what sinks her.
- **Climax:** on the drive to the final session in another city, a highway closure strands them
  overnight. In the stopped car she proposes that they withdraw and settle the sale question
  themselves, and they do. Staging: road or weather / the two of them / no evidence / her own
  choice / a private line.
- **Repair (dominant shape: changing a structure):** together they rewrite the founders' agreement,
  and he moves the company's headquarters into the factory.
- **Resolution:** reconciled. **Ending image (document or record):** the rewritten clause, in two
  handwritings.
- **Shape triple:** commission · changing a structure · document or record.

## Candidate Z3

- **POV / time:** three narrators (wife, husband, the new executive), close third, past tense. Opens
  at the handover.
- **Bind:** she refuses to cede the office and moves her own desk into it. The wife and the "work
  wife" share one room, in forced proximity, under a product-launch deadline.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 2):** she carries her desk in, in front of the staff.
- **Midpoint:** sharing the room, the two women compare notes and find the husband has told each of
  them a different version of the company's future.
- **Lowest point:** the executive's product launch fails publicly, and the husband blames the
  executive to the board to save face. He does to her what he did to his wife, and the wife watches.
- **Repair (dominant shape: public confession):** he tells the board and the staff that the failure
  and the erasure were his.
- **Climax:** the two women jointly present an alternative to the sale to the board. Staging: a public
  meeting / an institution / documents read aloud / a vote / the room turns audibly.
- **Resolution:** reconciled. The executive leaves for a better job. **Ending image:** a spoken line.
- **Shape triple:** commission · public confession · spoken line.
