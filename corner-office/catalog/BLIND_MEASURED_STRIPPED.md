# Measured fingerprint: the finished manuscript, surface-stripped (Continuity Audit)

**Declared constants** (owner's packaging; reported, not counted under H7): the husband (CEO)
publicly gives the wife's founding office to a newly hired senior executive he leans on
emotionally, with no physical affair, and says she "barely uses it". She co-founded the company
and owns a third. The boardroom learns whose idea everything was. He begs. They reconcile.

The company's product is a room built in a factory and lifted into place by crane. The office is
the first one ever built, made by her before the company existed.

- **POV / time:** two first-person narrators in present tense, about 70% hers. **Opens in medias
  res:** at night, a crane lifts the office out of headquarters. Chapter 2 goes back twenty hours.
- **Bind:** a pending sale to a large acquirer needs 75% of shareholders, so her third is a veto. A
  lock-up bars her from selling her shares. She cannot sell or leave the company, and the sale
  cannot happen without her.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 1–3):** overnight she has the office craned out into the
  factory yard. She posts the video herself the next morning. Cost: board censure, an acquirer
  "founder risk" flag, a city fine, and the husband humiliated in front of the acquirer.
- **Midpoint (Ch 15, boardroom):** the viral video has produced thousands of deposits for her
  original design. Deposits sit in escrow until steel is cut, under her own founding rule. A
  diligence question about the inventor gets the husband's one-line answer: hers. She then corrects the acquirer herself, on the record, listing what else is hers (the knee, the crane bay, the escrow rule, the name). The finance chief
  then reveals nine weeks of cash and that the husband secretly pledged his shares to a lender to
  make payroll. Her veto now starves the demand her own stunt created. She asks for thirty days.
  The censure fails 2–2 because the husband votes against it.
- **Ally turn:** she asks the executive for help; they build an independent plan (supplier terms, a
  state training grant, escrow released at steel-cut).
- **Lowest point. Her own act:** pressured by a planted press leak and a frightened crew, she goes on
  live TV and announces no sale, telling no one first. The acquirer walks. The lender pulls the
  covenant test forward to ten business days. The executive resigns over a broken arrangement with
  Pip, not as a mirror of the husband. In the fight, no one says she did "his thing". He says only
  that he would have said yes if she'd called; she throws the 1 a.m. calls at him and sends him out.
  He goes to sleep in the removed office. The book does not rebalance blame: in Ch 22 he looks for
  a version where the last two years aren't his fault and doesn't find one.
- **Repair (dominant shape: accepting a consequence he could avoid).** Rhythm: he tells her
  beforehand, does it unwatched, and she does not verify. He originates the practice himself after a
  plant manager's line about "finding out from the parking lot"; she never sets terms. Acts:
  - he ends the late-night calls with the executive himself;
  - he goes to the lender alone and signs an unlimited personal guarantee so his assets go first, in
    exchange for a single test date and a right of first refusal that keeps his shares away from the
    acquirer (the lender agrees for business reasons);
  - he apologises in full, once, kneeling, with no audience;
  - in public he proposes closing his own second plant, his project and his mistake, to spare the
    crew their Saturdays.
- **Climax (heroine-caused):** at 6 a.m. shift change on the factory floor she puts her plan to a
  workforce vote: Saturdays plus 5% of the company from her shares into a crew trust. He stands up
  and disagrees: close his plant instead. They argue as equals. She decides on the spot, writes his
  plant's name in the empty box on the whiteboard herself, and calls the vote. The tally is 97–41–3;
  the crew's oldest lead votes no, openly, arguing that a failed plan might bring a buyer's cheque back. Night before: the lead refuses to give the heroine advice, and the heroine texts the husband ahead of time that she won't cut him off. Staging: workplace floor /
  workforce / no evidence / a vote / a quiet tally.
- **Concealment motive:** pride. He hid the loan because she had warned him the second plant was
  wrong, and telling her meant saying she'd been right. ("I'd rather owe Chicago nine million dollars
  than owe her that sentence.")
- **Kneeling apology (Ch 25) and confession:** asked why he gave the room away, he answers: he
  couldn't bear walking past the room where they were married, dark, as proof of what they'd become,
  so he gave it to the woman who asked. No secret devotion, no reveal that she stopped first. The
  blame stays his.
- **Resolution (Ch 29–30):** in the same week, she asks him into the room at seven, where the ritual
  stopped. He asks to come home, clear the room with the ladder, and come out every morning at seven
  before driving downtown to his own job. On Friday the lender's test passes by $410k. The crew
  writes the date on the whiteboard. No epilogue, no months-later jump, no shared desk.
  **Final image (object at rest):** his chair, pushed back from the radiator at an angle where he
  left it that morning; she doesn't straighten it.
- **Shape triple:** commission · accepting a consequence he could avoid · object at rest.
- **Supporting cast:** the crew's older lead wants the acquirer's retention money, says so, votes
  against the heroine, and refuses to give her advice. The executive leaves for a job in another city.
  The heroine's living father supplies the rule's meaning ("nobody finds out alone").
