# Blind batch: three candidate concepts (surface-stripped)

Names, places, company names and the title are replaced with placeholders. Professions are
described only by what they make possible in the plot. All three share one fixed hook from the
packaging (axis 1): **the husband, who is CEO, publicly gives the wife's founding office to a
newly hired senior executive (a woman he leans on emotionally; no physical affair). Asked why,
he says she barely uses it. The wife co-founded the company and owns a third of it.** In all
three the office is a physical prototype she built before the company existed. The company's
product is a room that is built in a factory and lifted into place by crane, so the office can
be removed in one night.

---

## Candidate X1

- **POV / time:** two first-person narrators in present tense, mostly hers. **Opens in medias res:**
  at night, during her retaliation, a crane is lifting the office out of headquarters. Chapter 2
  goes back to the morning of the handover.
- **Bind:** the company is being sold to a large acquirer that would close the local factory and
  cheapen her design. The sale needs a 75% shareholder vote, so her third is a veto. Leaving the
  company or the marriage does not dissolve the veto, and walking away would abandon a workforce
  her late father helped train. She stays as the veto. The cost is daily proximity to both of them
  and board pressure.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 1–3):** overnight she has the office unbolted and lifted by
  crane into the factory yard. The price: a staffer's video goes viral, the board moves to censure
  her, the acquirer flags "founder risk", and the husband is humiliated in front of the acquirer.
- **Midpoint (about 50%, in the boardroom):** the meeting is called to censure her. The new
  executive, asked to rebut her, tells the board that every core idea in the product is the wife's.
  The boardroom learns whose idea everything was from the rival's mouth. Then the investor reveals
  that the company is nine weeks from missing payroll and the husband has quietly pledged his own
  shares to cover it. Her veto stops being a shield and becomes a weapon against her own workforce.
- **Ally turn:** the wife and the new executive build a smaller-company plan together. It would
  close the husband's prized second factory.
- **Lowest point:** the husband secretly extends the acquirer's exclusivity as a fallback and asks
  the executive to keep it from his wife. She refuses, resigns, and tells the wife. The wife also
  learns what he confided to the executive and never to her: why he missed her father's funeral.
  She asks him to leave the house.
- **Repair (dominant shape: accepting a consequence he could avoid):** he moves into the removed
  office in the factory yard, among a night crew he never knew. He sits through her family's Sunday
  dinner, where her brothers despise him, and takes it. He flies to his second factory and tells
  sixty workers himself that it is closing, a job he would always have delegated. He withdraws the
  fallback and tells the board the cash crisis was his doing. His rule through all of it is that he
  does not pitch; he only answers. The witnesses are the crew, her family and the board.
- **Climax (heroine-caused):** at 6 a.m. shift change on the factory floor, she asks the workforce
  to vote on her plan: a six-month pay deferral in exchange for equity carved from her own third.
  The husband is asked to speak and declines to sell it: "It's her plan. I'm in." Hands go up and
  are counted. Staging: workplace floor / the workforce / no evidence / a vote / a quiet tally.
- **Resolution:** full reconciliation, no epilogue. The last scene, days later: the office is lifted
  into their own back garden at home. **Ending image (object at rest):** two mugs on her father's
  desk inside it, snow on the roof.
- **Shape triple:** inciting = commission · repair = accepting a consequence he could avoid · ending image = object at rest.

## Candidate X2

- **POV / time:** single first-person narrator, past tense. Opens at the handover.
- **Bind:** the founders' agreement has a buy-sell ("shotgun") clause. Once she triggers it, it
  cannot be withdrawn, and a 30-day clock runs.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 4):** she triggers the clause and names a price for a third. He
  must buy her out at that price or sell his stake to her. Either way, one of them leaves the company.
- **Midpoint:** to everyone's shock he elects to sell to her. Now she has to raise the money or lose
  everything. She wins the company and may lose all of it.
- **Lowest point:** she discovers he chose to sell because the new executive invited him to co-found
  a new venture with her in another city. The emotional affair has become a career elopement.
- **Repair (dominant shape: restitution):** he wires his buyout proceeds back into her company as
  capital with no control rights, and returns what he took.
- **Climax (heroine-caused):** at the closing table she has raised the money and rewrites the deal.
  Staging: document exchange / the two of them and counsel / documents / a contract / a private line.
- **Resolution:** a renegotiated marriage. She runs the company and he works elsewhere. **Ending
  image:** a spoken line.
- **Shape triple:** commission · restitution · spoken line.

## Candidate X3

- **POV / time:** two third-person narrators, past tense. Opens at the handover.
- **Bind:** the shared house and the marriage, plus a non-compete.
- **First irreversible decision (Ch 3):** she resigns and takes the chief design job at the acquirer
  across the river. The price: a non-compete threat, and the workforce sees her as a traitor.
- **Midpoint:** she learns the acquirer hired her as bait to buy her old company cheaply.
- **Lowest point:** on the new executive's advice, her husband signs a lawsuit against her.
- **Repair (dominant shape: letting her be right in front of the people who mattered):** he
  withdraws the suit on the record and testifies that the designs are hers in front of the
  acquirer's board.
- **Climax (heroine-caused):** her testimony in mediation, with the original design records produced.
  Staging: a mediation room / an institution / documents read out / an official's decision / the
  room turns audibly.
- **Resolution:** reconciled, working for rival employers. **Ending image:** a third party's gesture.
- **Shape triple:** commission · letting her be right · third party's gesture.
