## 3. Rulings on the two script hard failures (`similarity_architecture.md`)

**vs Candidate A ("only 3 of 7 primary axes differ"; 5/8 core by my count): LINEAGE, not sameness. Upheld, with conditions.**
- A2 is labelled in the pool as "a re-architecture of batch-1 A". A was the *selected* batch-1
  concept for this same book. It was superseded because it failed H3 alone, and it was never
  rejected as a duplicate of anything. Only one of A and A2 can ever become a published book,
  so a repeat reader cannot meet both. That reader-facing harm is what H1 exists to prevent.
- The axes ARCH keeps from A are the logline's draw, the rule bind, the truthful "no", the
  confession climax, and presence plus refusal. None of these caused A's H3 failure. The axes
  that changed are inciting *content* (she narrates the villain story about him), the first
  decision, the midpoint and the ending. These are exactly where her craft now does causal
  work, and H3 now passes.
- Conditions: (1) A must be filed in `SERIES_LEDGER.FINGERPRINTS` as
  `source: concept, rejected_for: superseded_by_A2 (H3)`, not as an independent option;
  (2) any future title that revives A's row must be scored as `recycled_reject` of *this book*
  (it would be 5/8 against it); (3) the lineage exemption covers A only, not the other pool
  rows.

**vs Candidate C ("same heroine goal and agency pattern with a hero-owned climax"): SCRIPT ARTIFACT. Overturned.**
- Two legs of the condition are true by function. Her goal is "keep the seasonal event alive"
  in both (C: the grandfather's event stays open; ARCH: hit the insurer's target so it
  survives). Her agency is heroine-led in both.
- The third leg is false. ARCH's climax belongs to the heroine: she unbuckles, walks out,
  takes the lantern and confesses, while he is silent and does not narrate. The drift fix
  (F1/F2) made this more true than at concept. The fail comes from the substring test
  `"hero" in owner` matching `heroine_led`, as the drift report documents.
- Core mechanism matches with C are 0/8 strict and staging is 1/5, so H1, H5 and H8 are all clear.
- Residual: "save the event by a season-end money target" is the heroine goal in A, A2 and C,
  and nearly so in E (clear a debt by season's end). This is convergence inside one book's
  pool, not a shelf repeat. It becomes a house-template item if a later title reuses the engine.

## 7. Risks a hostile repeat reader would hit (ranked)

- **R1 Ask/answer contract as the romance engine.** PRIOR-1 rebuilds the marriage on terms and
  lists of asks. ARCH runs on "ask me one thing each run; I'll answer true" and closes on "his one
  question". A repeat reader could write: "again a couple that fixes things by formal
  question-and-answer rules." Mitigation: keep the protocol his, keep it unevenly honoured,
  never formalize the ending as a new agreement, and keep the last chapter free of any ask.
- **R2 Hero's climax role is restraint again.** PRIOR-1: he stops where she says. ARCH: he stays
  belted and silent. Mitigation: dramatize his stillness as an active choice with a visible cost
  (for example, holding a frightened rider), and avoid "he did not" restraint narration in his
  POV around the climax.
- **R3 Self-report rhythm (T4).** He tells everything himself, twice. Mitigation: vary the
  delivery so that one disclosure is shown, not announced, without making it a discovered secret.
- **R4 "Original wrong recurs" at the lowest point.** Shared at class level with PRIOR-1, B, C
  and E. The heroine-agent flip is the only differentiator. Make "Go. You should go." read on the
  page as *her* pattern (the same words as years ago, quoted), not as a generic relapse.
- **R5 Care-object-and-service device.** The belt-buckling closes the climax. If it is the
  primary device, it repeats PRIOR-1 and perhaps the unavailable titles. Rotate the primary
  device in the Chemistry Ledger and record it in field 14.
- **R6 Her trade becomes the climax's proof at her own work site.** PRIOR-1 does this too, though
  there he performs it. The venue could be tagged "workplace". Keep it under 4/5; it is not a fail.
- **R7 Ending-image class ambiguity.** The raffle-ticket halves are a callback to the opening draw,
  which is E's class, and tickets also sit in D's last image and the title. If the measured
  fingerprint tags the ending "callback to the opening", the shape triple shares 2/3 with E.
  That is still no fail, but let the lantern carry the final sentence.
- **R8 The unavailable titles (cannot be scored; why the gate is BLOCKED).** The titles alone
  suggest public performance or humiliation in a hometown ("…Brought Her Onstage in My
  Hometown") and abandonment ("…Left Me Out in the Cold"). ARCH has a public performance about
  the hero in his hometown, a newspaper, and a performed reckoning. This could be an inversion
  collision that only those books' texts can confirm or clear. Under Never-list 3 I do not score
  from titles.
- **R9 Lineage hygiene.** Revival of A anywhere in the pen name is a 5/8 duplicate of this book.
- **R10 Pool engine convergence.** The "seasonal event survives a money ultimatum" goal appears in
  A, A2, C and nearly E. Record it so the next title does not reuse it.
