# Surface-stripped fingerprint: MEASURED-H (finished manuscript, full text; measured, not planned)

Lane: sweet small-town holiday second chance (closed door).

**Placeholders:**
- HER, HIM: the former fiancés. She ended the engagement seven years ago, weeks before the wedding.
- BROTHER: her much younger brother, whom she raised.
- ALLY: the older vice-chair who loves HER and has repeated HER version for years.
- ELDER: the old woman who owns the land.
- [TOWN]: the small town.
- [EVENT]: the town's annual ten-night volunteer-run attraction. Paying riders sit on a wagon; it ends with a story told at a pond. HER rules govern it and a committee votes on its script.

**Roles described by what they make possible:**
- HER [EVENT] role: she builds its effects and wrote its finale story. This lets her own the public narrative and makes her the only person who can withdraw it. The finale is a stage she controls.
- HER day job: she works with a roomful of early teenagers. This supplies a chorus that asks the true question out loud.
- HIS trade: he builds things for shows that fake their weight. This lets him make the new finale technically possible and gives him a want (a shop of his own). His old employer's collapse is why he came back, and a big-city offer is what creates the goal collision.

---

1. **Inciting:** choice under pressure. At a public supper, HER own laminated rules govern a random draw that pairs her with HIM as front-bench partners for all ten nights: no swaps, a partner who leaves the draw leaves the season, and a chair who breaks a rule steps down. ALLY offers a redraw in front of the town, and she cites her rule instead. For six years the finale has cast HIM as the villain who abandoned a waiting bride. On the first ride the riders realise "that's the guy" and turn to stare at him.
2. **Bind:** her own rules, plus a cash target the [EVENT] must reach by a fixed date or it loses its insurance and ends. The villain story is what sells. The on-page cost: she tells the story beside him seven times a night while crowds stare and heckle. When she softens one line, the committee votes her a formal warning 4–1; one more breach and she steps down. HE stays by choice and works the cue box for his own villain role.
3. **First irreversible decision:** at 11 of 30, she withdraws her own story mid-season under an "author may withdraw" clause ("It's just a pond"). The price is refunds, a visible revenue drop and ALLY's hurt ("Is this for him?").
4. **Midpoint:** at 14 of 30, the lie is withdrawn and a true communal story replaces it. ELDER gives HER a true story about the night a lost child was searched for by a line of neighbours with lanterns. HER asks the owner before the committee; the committee adopts it 4–3. The couple co-build it in HIS workspace, and it works on first performance. Costs: ALLY breaks with her, and the old story goes to the press.
5. **Rupture:** HER core flaw recurs on a goal collision. Her last question gets a true answer: HE would not have come back if his job hadn't ended. He then discloses a big-city offer in person and says he is undecided. She says "Go" again, exactly as she did seven years ago. He refuses to let her decide for him. BROTHER names her pattern: she says "go" first so that leaving is her idea. The local paper prints ALLY's false version on the front page.
6. **Climax:** led by HER. On the final ride she breaks the belt rule, walks the dock mid-finale, and lifts the unveiled lantern from her old effect. To the riders she retells the original story truthfully: HE asked her to come, then asked her to wait, and she sent him. "She was never waiting. She sent him." A child asks "Is that true?" A rider asks where the groom is. HE answers from the bench, still belted, by reversing the ghost story's line: "Right here. Choosing this one." Afterwards she enforces the step-down penalty on herself, and ALLY says "I was wrong."
7. **Repair:** an inverted grovel.
   - **HIS side:** sustained truthful presence. He apologises and asks for nothing. He gives one true answer a night, including answers that cost him: the ring was sold, and not calling was "easy". He never defends himself in public. He gives care without fixing (holding the light while she repairs her own rig). He declines the offer for his own reasons and reports its real cost.
   - **HER side:** a staged public correction. A softened line that fails, then the withdrawal, then a private "I'm sorry", then the dock confession, then the self-imposed step-down. Her brother urges a one-word reply ("stay"); she refuses it and states her own terms instead ("when you ask, ask on a Tuesday ... the answer's yes"). She fixes her own long-broken porch light alone, the night before the old wedding date.
8. **Resolution:** full reconciliation. They say "I love you". He announces a proposal is coming. She answers in her own terms (ask on an ordinary day, without a crowd; the answer will be yes), and he writes the day in the frost. There is no time skip: the epilogue is the next-morning teardown of the [EVENT]. Last image: the unveiled, unlit lantern hung on an empty barn nail, still.

**Staging:**
- venue: the stage of a live performance (dock and wagon at the finale stop)
- audience: 32 paying riders, mostly strangers, plus ALLY and a child
- evidence: confession
- authority: HER own choice (she breaks the rule, then enforces its penalty on herself)
- spectacle: silent; the room turns without a sound, then lanterns are lifted toward the bench

**Shape triple:** choice under pressure / public confession (inverted: heroine-side; hero-side sustained presence) / object at rest.

**Macro beats:**

**Primary devices:**
- **First attraction beat:** a true first-inspection line on the body ("looks like somebody let him out of a coat"), capped by an object-act (he brought a pin for her ticket).
- **First care beat:** not a bank device. It is restraint as care: he stands still to give her a second to withdraw, and offers her a cover story.
- **Later care:** care-object-and-service appears once (a held light while she repairs her own rig); the back half carries care through truthful disclosure and his own shop, not service to her house.
- **Secondary devices:** others'-talk introduction, explicit answer-length ladder, mirror lines ("choosing the city" → "choosing this one"; "go" answered by her own terms, not by a one-word reversal), charm-witnesses, comic undercut exits, narrator-wrong-once, a weak involuntary symptom, pinned ticket halves as a token.
- **Question engine:** one true answer per night, asked in the dark on the ride home.

**Voices:**
- **HER:** close third past. Attention goes to rooms and timing (where people are looking, building the wait). Effects-craft metaphors. A controlled face ("kept her face exactly where it was"). Aphoristic chapter openers. Some chapters end on her own handwritten note on the script.
- **HIM:** close third past. Calls his own life like stage cues ("Standby humiliation. Humiliation, go"; "Blackout"), talks to objects, self-mocking hyperbole in parentheses, things lying about their weight. Restraint written as actions not taken is low ("He didn't…", 11× sentence-initial). Filter-word rate below threshold.
- **Shared:** an ensemble of side characters, each with a fixed catchphrase. Many chapters end on a side character's punchline. Dense callbacks. Exact times and money figures. Near-zero em dashes. Low gloss.
