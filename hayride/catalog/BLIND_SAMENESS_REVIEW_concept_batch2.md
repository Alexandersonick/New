## Method

- **Core axes (8)** are the eight macro-beats: inciting, bind, first irreversible decision (FID), midpoint, rupture, climax, repair and resolution. They are matched by mechanism (what causes the beat and who acts), not by label.
- **Partial** marks a mechanism echo with a different agent or function. I counted partials toward the totals so the totals err on the strict side, and I show them separately.
- **Logline-forced elements** are not counted as sameness unless a candidate builds on them. These elements are the same in every row: exes, he left for [CITY], he is back to stay and apologetic, the [DRAW] pairs them for all 10 runs, and the scares are staged.
- **Shape triple:** inciting kind / repair shape / ending-image class.
- **Staging (5):** venue / audience / evidence / authority / spectacle.

## Hostile-romance-reader weaknesses

**A2**
- *Unlikeable heroine (HIGH):* she knowingly profits for five runs from a story she knows is unfair, with him sitting beside her, after letting the town blame him for years. Readers will bail if her cost is not on the page every run.
- *Reckless climax:* "unbelts and leaves the bench mid-run, breaking crew safety." If the vehicle is moving with riders aboard, the heroine who leads an insurer-threatened event endangers the public. That reads as incompetence, not courage.
- *A climax that drifts to him:* "he… takes over the narration." If his voice carries the story at her moment, the climax reads as half his, or as him publicly subordinating himself to her (template element 4).
- *Hero who sinks:* across runs 1–8 he sits, answers and does grunt work. Without his own drive he reads as a penitent doormat.
- *Rupture blame:* if she punishes him for an honest answer *she* asked for, she reads as petty.
- Passive heroine: no. Repair that is just a speech: no.

**D**
- Passive inciting (a committee's clerical omission happens to her).
- Low-cost private climax (a talk in a maze); readers will say "nothing happens."
- Long-distance HEA reads as HFN to many romance readers.
- Repair is cash (he sells his truck to cover her rent), which can read as "he buys the ending."
- His original wrong (leaving) is never repaired; the logline's apology is vestigial.
- The [EVENT] is wallpaper.

**E**
- Climax is really his (he withdraws and gives her the stage).
- Repair is just a speech (public confession).
- Community bailout via the bid sheet.
- Heroine's goal is money via a popularity contest: she pressures him to fake love, then blames him for refusing, which makes her unlikeable.

## Ruling on the script's A2 FAIL (vs. rejected C)

**OVERTURNED.** The core-axis count is **0/8**, by named mechanism:

| Axis | A2 | C |
|---|---|---|
| Inciting | chance pairing locked by **her own** rule | **commission**: his regulatory act against her |
| Bind | self-authored rule → a rival takes her post | regulation makes her depend on **him** as officer |
| FID | moral complicity (profits from an unfair story) | signed personal legal liability |
| Midpoint | voluntary costly renunciation; **no reveal** | **secret motive uncovered** by investigation |
| Rupture | his **truthful answer**, plus an exit he does not take | he **repeats his wrong** (duty over her), publicly |
| Climax | her self-indictment; forfeits post; **no ratifier** | her engineered plan, **ratified by his sign-off** + weather |
| Repair | **unwitnessed** private refusal, told plainly | **public** on-duty reversal before witnesses |
| Resolution | object at rest; no epilogue | act in motion, next season |

1. **"Hero-owned climax" is a script artifact.** `climax_owner = heroine_led` contains the substring "hero". The value literally says heroine-led, and the beat confirms it: she leaves the bench and confesses while he stays seated. C's climax is also heroine-led. The script should test equality (`== "hero_led"`) or use a token match, not a substring.
2. **"Same heroine goal and agency pattern" is half-true and non-dispositive.** Both heroines want to keep the [EVENT] alive under institutional threat. That is a real goal-category echo, and it is shared with old A, so it is a house lean. The agency pattern is inverted. C's heroine *complies*: she engineers a plan that satisfies the hero's terms, and he ratifies it. A2's heroine *forfeits*: she breaks her own rule and gives up the post and the funding story, and nobody ratifies it.
3. **The serial-shape overlap (5/7) is mostly logline- and pipeline-forced defaults:**
   - *exes:* the logline fixes it in all 6 rows.
   - *early costed commitment:* the pipeline requires it (beat 3) in every row; the mechanisms differ (complicity vs. liability signature).
   - *no relocation:* forced by the logline's "he is back to stay"; shared by A, A2, B, C and E.
   - *heroine-led climax:* the pipeline's heroine-agency requirement; shared by A2, B and C, with D and E joint.
   - *community-witnessed resolution:* the **only real choice**, and it is partial. A2's climax is witnessed by a vehicle-load of riders, but its resolution (the teardown morning) is private. The same element is shared with B, C and E, so it is a house lean to watch rather than an A2–C match.

The script FAIL does not stand. Recommend that the script exclude logline-fixed elements from the serial-shape count and fix the substring test.

### Changes A2 still needs before the Concept Kill Test

1. **Make the climax safe.** Her exit happens at a scheduled, protocol stop at the effect site, with the vehicle halted and riders secure. What she breaks is her own rule and the bench, not rider safety.
2. **Keep the climax in her voice.** He keeps the riders seated and runs the vehicle. He does **not** narrate, continue or "correct" her story; at most he gives one logistics line. Cut "takes over the narration" or confine it to non-story ride patter. This guards against "the climax is really his" and against template element 4.
3. **No ratifier and no community bailout.** No insurer sign-off, no revealed sales tally, no rival defeated by vote, no crowd collection saving the season. Keep a real kept cost: she loses or hands over the post, or the target is missed and the event's future is settled by something already set up through her craft. Otherwise A2 slides into the B/C/E pattern of community money under a formal count.
4. **Relabel the spectacle.** Replace "quiet tally" (it is a loud performance in the dark), then rescore staging. Make sure the last image's "two raffle stubs" are a personal token (their two tickets), not a count of sales.
5. **Put her cost on the page in runs 1–4.** Every run she tells the story, her private cost must be visible, plus at least one costed attempt to soften it within the rules. Give the reader a reason, on page, to stay with her before run 5.
6. **Disclose the exit offer directly.** She must learn of the rehire offer from him, or by witnessing it, at the rupture. Never through a third party or an overheard call, which would rebuild secret-then-discovered. Specify who breaks at the rupture and how, so she is not punishing an honest answer she asked for.
7. **Give him agency beyond penance.** Give him a reason of his own in POV for offering the bench and the question device, and one active want that is not atonement, so he does not sink.
8. **Hold the de-templated elements during drafting.** Keep the question device as his offer, the midpoint as a choice rather than a reveal, the repair unwitnessed with nothing discovered later, and no time-jump epilogue. The open goal-category echo with C (saving the [EVENT] from an institutional threat) may stay, because A2 inverts it into forfeiture. If the Kill Test wants more distance, anchor her goal in authorship of the story rather than event survival.
