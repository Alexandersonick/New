Shared setting skeleton (surface): a small farm town; a volunteer-run haunted hayride on Friday and Saturday nights through October (10 rides: Oct 2, 3, 9, 10, 16, 17, 23, 24, 30, 31). Each wagon carries two volunteer **wagon guides** belted onto the front bench. They narrate the town legend, keep riders seated, and cue the staged scares. Guide pairs are drawn by raffle at the September Harvest Supper, a decades-old tradition. Swaps are not allowed.

## Candidate A: "Ten Questions"

**Situation.** She is the hayride's chair and its scare builder. She engineers the effects: pulleys, fog, a bed-sheet ghost on a zip line. Last year she wrote the iron no-swap rule after a swapped-seats feud nearly split the volunteers. At the Supper she draws his name from the jar herself. Eight years ago he broke their engagement two weeks before the wedding and left for a city job. He is back, apologetic, and volunteering.

- **Her goal (non-romantic):** clear the season's ticket target. The insurer has given the hayride one more season to pay for a new wagon and the liability rider, or it closes.
- **Bind:** breaking her own rule in her first year as chair hands the chair to a rival, who wants to shut the hayride and turn the money over to a fall festival. Cost: her word in a town that keeps score.
- **First irreversible decision (Ride 1, ~Ch 3):** she sets terms on the front bench. Ten rides, ten questions, one per ride, answered truthfully. After Halloween he keeps to his side of town. He counters, and she accepts: he gets one question back at the end. The price is that she has bound herself to hear the answers.
- **Midpoint (Ride 5, ~Ch 14):** he spends his one question early, as the bargain allows: "Why didn't you come?" Her answer flips the town's story. He asked her to come with him. She refused and told him to go, then let the town tell it as abandonment for eight years. The fault becomes shared, and she is the one who has to decide whether to correct the record.
- **Rupture (Ride 9, ~Ch 24):** her ninth question: "Would you have come back if the job hadn't ended?" He answers truthfully: no. He came back because he was let go. Her own truth device makes the wound, and "back for good" now looks like circumstance, not choice.
- **Climax (Ride 10, Oct 31):** she rewrites the town legend mid-ride. Instead of the scripted tale, she narrates their true story to a full wagon, including her own part. Heroine-led. The wagon's ticket line, which has spread by word of mouth, carries the target over the line.
- **Repair:** sustained, unglamorous presence. Across the back half he does the dull work nobody sees, at cost: he turns down a city rehire offer that arrives in mid-October and is not told about the confession beforehand.
- **Ending:** full reconciliation. Coda: the next October's raffle, where the last image is a spoken line ("Question eleven.").

## Candidate B: "Two Bids"

**Situation.** She has driven the hayride tractor for twelve years and wants to buy the operation from the retiring owner, who sells on Nov 1 to whichever bidder runs the season better. At the Supper she draws her ex for the front bench. Then she discovers he is the other bidder. He came back for good with his city savings and means to buy it, believing it was what she always wanted him to build.

- **Her goal:** own the hayride and run it her way, with the farm's books and her own name on the sign.
- **Bind:** the owner's condition. Both bidders work every ride beside each other, and the raffle seat stands. Walking off means withdrawing.
- **First irreversible decision (~Ch 4):** she puts her house up as collateral for the bid. Visible cost: the bank sign-off, and her sister's fury.
- **Midpoint (~Ch 14):** the owner's daughter, sympathetic and short of money, moves to sell the land outright to a seasonal corn-maze franchise for cash. Both bids become moot unless they merge. A joint goal forms.
- **Rupture (~Ch 24):** the merger requires his money in front of her name. That reruns the original wound, when he made the big decision for both of them. Goal collision: she walks out of the partnership.
- **Climax (Oct 31):** at the owner's kitchen table, with the family present, she produces a bid built from town pre-sold season passes plus her equity. Heroine-led, a document exchange. The contract decides it.
- **Repair:** relinquishing control. He converts his money into a silent minority share under her sole management, and in front of the family he signs away his voting rights.
- **Ending:** new partnership, business and romantic. Coda: the new sign and the charter (a document or record) at next season's opening.

## Candidate C: "The Inspector"

**Situation.** She runs the hayride, which her late grandfather founded. He left eight years ago to become a city firefighter. He is back for good as the newly appointed county fire marshal, and he volunteers as a wagon guide to rejoin the town. The raffle seats them together. The day after the Supper, in his first official act, he red-tags the hayride's creek bridge. The hayride can run only with a certified fire officer on every wagon and the bridge repaired by the third weekend.

- **Her goal:** keep her grandfather's hayride open, and prove the town is not a fire hazard run on nostalgia.
- **Bind:** he is the certified officer on her wagon. The permit is conditional on him riding. Trading him out shuts down her own wagon.
- **First irreversible decision (~Ch 3):** she signs the corrective-action plan with herself as the responsible party and commits to rebuilding the bridge in two weeks with volunteers.
- **Midpoint (~Ch 14):** secret motive exposed. He left after the barn fire the night of their engagement party. The original hayride barn burned with no one hurt. He believes his lantern started it, and he has never told her. He became a firefighter because of it. The town's story ("he left for ambition") is wrong, and so is his ("it was my fault"). Her investigation of the old fire report shows the cause was the wiring her grandfather knew about.
- **Rupture (~Ch 24):** systemic cost. A storm warning on Oct 31 makes him close the final night's route on duty. He chooses safety over her, in public, exactly as he once chose leaving over her.
- **Climax (Oct 31):** she re-routes the hayride on foot. A lantern walk crosses the rebuilt bridge on a route she plans to pass his storm conditions. The town walks it in the wind. Heroine-led. Venue: the road and the weather. Authority: the weather, and his sign-off.
- **Repair:** letting her be right in front of the people who mattered. On duty, in public, he reverses his own closure on her plan's merits and says so to the crowd.
- **Ending:** reconciliation and engagement. Last image: an act in motion, their wagon crossing the bridge on next year's first ride.
