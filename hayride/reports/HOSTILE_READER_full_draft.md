### same_book_review_draft (128 words)

> ★★☆☆☆ **Same couple, new costume.** Swap the restoration job for a haunted hayride and the evil twin for a ghost story, and it's the identical engine. A brilliant, number-counting heroine who talks in one-word verdicts ("Belt." "Flashlight."). A hero who wins her back by standing silently holding the light while she fixes things, not touching anything, "he didn't, he didn't, he didn't." Sweet, but I've read this couple before.

### De-fingerprinting fixes (line and scene level, no premise change)

1. **Cut "He didn't" by about half** and never stack three in a row. This also fixes C2.
2. **Invert the silent-hands axis once, on the page.** The hitch-pin rescue (Ch13) already does this, with Gus leading and talking and Roz holding Pete's slicker. Protect it and add one more: Gus leads a build and Roz is the hands. Options are the Ch26 door hang (Roz rather than Danny holds the door, or she arrives for the second hinge) or Gus teaching her something in his barn.
3. **Ch15 soldering lesson.** Make the teaching two-way. For example, Gus shows her how a flat "lies about its weight" (L1643) and she uses it on the reeds rig. That way it is not master and apprentice.
4. **Thin Roz's exact-number interiority by about a third** (L1609, L2153, L3105, L4041, L5143). Keep the science-teacher flavor; lose the forensic tic.
5. **Loosen the "official watching" axis in the climax.** Let Bev's ruling in Ch29 come from the floor (Dot, or the room) rather than from the vice-chair with a clipboard. Or move Bev's "I was wrong" out of a formal handbook-and-ruling frame.
6. **Keep "Stay."** It is the book's thematic payoff. Do not add another one-word spoken button near it (cut or move Hank's closing "Yup." in Ch29 L5157).
