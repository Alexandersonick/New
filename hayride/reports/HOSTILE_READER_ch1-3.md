**Why `true` while O9 passes:** the *opening* axes (disruption, decision, entry point) are new. The *engine* axes are the ones a repeat reader remembers: penance as silent service in her craft, an act on her spoken cue, an official approval body, a deadline object, and the precise-heroine / negating-hero voice pairing. Those are already on the page by Ch3, and the reader can project the PRIOR-1 back half onto them.

**Opening-level mitigations (without an outline):**
1. Break "silent." Gus's penance should be talk: the truth-a-night game should drive the book, not the whistle. Cut or soften "I won't say anything" (L607).
2. Break "on her count." Let the whistle be his timing, which she had never been able to get from anyone. The line is already half there at L547. Do not make her spoken number the mechanism. Remove "on *ten*" as the repeated signature, or make the timing his choice rather than her count.
3. Thin Roz's measurement tics. Keep 2–3 signature precisions and cut the rest (L59 "half an inch", L537 "to the second", L697 "to the foot").
4. Make sure the back half does not stage "he flips FOG alone and wrecks it" plus a committee-witnessed joint finale. Upstream should check this against the outline; this reader has not seen it.

## Requested flags

### AI texture (stock phrasing, over-explaining, the narrator stating meaning)
- **L211:** "He'd brought a pin. He had come to the Supper knowing that if his name came out of the jar beside anyone's, he'd be pinning them over his heart, and he had come anyway, with a pin." The narrator explains the gesture the reader just felt, and the logic is off (everyone pins, so the pin isn't about her). Cut to "He'd brought a pin."
- **L221:** "There it was, the first crack." This labels the beat. Cut it; "it landed on her like a dropped sandbag" already does the work.
- **L195:** "He was giving her one more second… He was building her an exit. He was standing very still so that…" Rule-of-three anaphora that explains the gesture. Keep one sentence.
- **L268:** "It had sounded ridiculous. It had also sounded like his." A tidy reversal couplet with a template feel.
- **L547:** "the way it was supposed to and had not, in six Octobers, ever once come, because every guide who'd ever sat… had flipped it late, a half-beat or a full beat after the line, and she had made her peace…" Over-explains a beat that "It came on *ten*" already landed. Trim by half.
- **Aphorism count (craft maxims stated by the narrator):** L59 "the trick of a room was the trick of a scare", L433 "A scare is just a promise you keep two seconds late", L503 "You built the waiting, and then you broke it", L521 "people leaned in to a quiet voice and leaned back from a loud one". Each is good, and four in three chapters is the limit. Don't add a fifth.
- **Simile load:** 15 "like a…" and 16 "the way…" constructions, with repeats (see HR-09).

### Heroine unlikeable without the narrative acknowledging it
- **Ch3 L551–559** (primary). Roz says "Still choosing the city" with him beside her, then registers only the damage to her cue. She needs one honest interior line acknowledging that it is cruel and that she is choosing it anyway. For example, after L561: *She'd written that line in 2020 for a man who wasn't there. She said it six more times that night to one who was, and she knew exactly what that made her, and she said it anyway.* The narrator naming the flaw first takes it out of the reviewer's hands.
- **Ch3 L461:** "she had decided… that she would not laugh at anything he said for ten nights." This reads fine; it is self-aware. Keep it.
- **Ch1 overall:** her coldness is unmotivated on the page until Ch2 (HR-02).

### Who-is-who confusion
- August / Gus / Augie: three names inside Ch1 L85–231 with no bridge at L195.
- Rosalind / Roz / Rosie / Miss P. This is fine, because each name is tied to who is speaking.
- Danny: the brother reveal is delayed (L47 vs. L115).
- Marguerite is a tractor and Delores a truck. Both are clarified in-line, which is OK, but add to the noun count.
- The month "August" vs. the hero August (HR-05).
- Hank is mentioned in Ch1 L217 ("Hank won't wait for you") before he is introduced in Ch2.
- Walt, Roz's father, appears only via Ruth (L278). Readers will ask whether he is dead. Seed it once in Roz's POV (the barn coat, L221).

### Is the hero on the page early enough?
Yes for the gate; marginal for the impatient reader. He is the felt absence from word 135, named at ~860, but physically on the page only at ~1,975 (77% through Ch1). He is excellent once present. Cutting ~300 words from L103–177 brings his walk-up to ~1,650.

### Promise check against the logline
- "Apologetic": no apology on the page in Ch1–3. His remorse is enacted (the exit offer, the whistle, "the least I could do"), which is fine, but sweet readers will want one spoken "I'm sorry" or a deliberate refusal to say it cheaply, soon.
- "The second look he keeps giving her": not rendered. In Roz's POV he only looks straight ahead. Give her one caught glance in Ch3 (the yard-light walk back, around L673).
