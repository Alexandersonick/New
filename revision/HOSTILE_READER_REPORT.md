# Hostile Reader Pass: revised manuscript

An independent reader did this pass architecture-blind: no outline, no audit, no edit list. They read chapters 1–30 in full and scored complaints against the review-cause ranks in `reader-contract-gates.md` §4. The repeat-reader briefing is **BLOCKED** because the earlier titles' fingerprints are unavailable. The pass was run on the revision before the "never once" and gloss follow-up edits, which were made in response to it.

**Verdict: PASS.** The reader finished willingly. No complaint maps above low severity to ranks 1, 2, 3, 9, 11 or 13. The medium risks are both rank 6 (promise and positioning): a lighter grovel than the niche expects, and a closed door under a melodrama title. These belong to the blurb, not the text.

## Explained symbolism noted
Mostly single clauses, in character, inside confrontations. The reader judged about five truly redundant, including Ch 23's internal version and Ch 27's "so nobody would ever see where she had been struck". *Both were cut in the follow-up.*

## Plausibility snags (all low)
- Declan never flew to Italy. The book owns this (the Valley Forge confession), but some readers will balk.
- Maud didn't check her email, voicemail or bank account for about 14 weeks.
- The impersonation fooled the neighbours. Judged mostly plausible.
- An amateur works on an altarpiece face and it gets certified. Flagged "irregular"; conservator readers may object (R05).
- A tidy denouement (Ray keeps him on with a hard 5:30); the backed-out screw is convenient to the plot.

## Agency and equality
Heroine agency is high: she sets the terms, confronts people, ends the arrangement, decides "LEAVE", and makes the final ask. The repair is behaviour-based: the hinged confession page, "my hand's tired", "Ask Maud", refusing to scapegoat Cora, staying away when told. The ending is reciprocal: his ask answers hers, and "we decide it". The secret Atlanta decision is named as "the corner again, done kindly" and is not endorsed.

## What the reader could not complain about
A page-one hook shown in scene; clean prose with no continuity slips found; dates and weekdays check out; one labelled POV per chapter; no cheating; the conflict is owned by both spouses rather than resting on a miscommunication; the heroine causes both the midpoint and the climax; the hero's change is shown in behaviour; the climax is a full scene with an external clock, followed by a real denouement; a distinct supporting cast with humour that lands; the sister and mother arcs keep their consequences; a reciprocal HEA.
